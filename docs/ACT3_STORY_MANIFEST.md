## 1. Chapter boundary

Act 2 ends with Barnet and Alve leaving the restored lake place in the restored motorboat.

The final formal Act 2 sequence is:

`Efter motorbåten -> Någon är där -> De kom -> Min kompis -> Det är bättre -> Över sjön -> SLUT PÅ ANDRA KAPITLET`

Act 3 owns:
- what is on the other side of the lake;
- the arrival;
- the city;
- Nova's story.

The first formal Act 3 beat is the black title card:

> **KAPITEL 3**  
> **På andra sidan sjön**

The transition must feel like a direct continuation of the boat departure, not a menu reset.

## 2. Setting — locked

Act 3 takes place in a **larger Swedish lakeside town/city**.

It is:
- clearly more urban than Act 1 and Act 2;
- not a metropolis;
- intimate, walkable and child-scale;
- built along the lake.

Visual/world vocabulary may include:
- compact town centre near the harbour;
- lakeside promenade;
- older town blocks;
- newer apartment areas;
- school;
- library;
- shops;
- bus stops;
- bicycles, buses, pedestrians and background city life.

The city should feel populated and active. This contrasts with Alve's quiet/abandoned Act 2 environment.

Nova's life is split between **two homes in different parts of the city**. Everyday details may quietly show that split life: duplicate chargers, toothbrushes, keys, weekly schedules and a room that feels more provisional in one home.

Nova's phone and headphones are established character props. They can support the story naturally but must not be over-symbolized.

## 3. Central Act 3 place — locked direction

The central story/play space sits by the water and is a **city park / folkpark / public lakeside recreation area** tied to Nova's family memories.

Possible established visual language includes:
- small café or pavilion;
- minigolf;
- playground;
- bathing jetty;
- grill area;
- small stage.

The place is **not an abandoned ruin**. It still functions, but it is worn and has lost some of the character Nova remembers.

Nova remembers spending good family days there before her parents separated.

The child-facing practical objective is initially simple:

**Make the place and the family day better and better.**

The emotional objective underneath is:

**Nova wants to recreate a perfect enough family day that her parents become a couple again.**

## 4. Core trio

### Barnet
Barnet is the player anchor and the person Nova can speak most openly with.

Barnet does not solve Nova's realization for her.

### Nova
Nova is the emotional protagonist of Act 3.

Established visual traits include:
- headphones;
- phone in back pocket;
- recurring canonical character sheet in the Art Pipeline.

Her parents have separated.

Nova has connected the separation to a day when she had been unusually angry/difficult. She therefore carries the child-logic:

**If being bad caused it, being perfect might undo it.**

This creates her perfection drive through the act.

Hard story rule:
**Nova's parents do not reunite.**

### Alve
Alve remains part of the core trio but is not the Act 3 protagonist.

He is:
- sidekick;
- friend;
- practical catalyst;
- funny and impulsive;
- subtly changed by Act 2.

Alve recognizes Nova's attempt to rebuild the past because he did something related in Act 2. He may notice the pattern before Barnet does, but must not become a miniature therapist or steal Nova's realization.

Approved continuity beat when Nova resists help:

> **Nova:** “Jag behöver ingen hjälp.”  
> **Alve:** “Du behöver inte göra allt själv.”

Alve may eventually describe how things at the lake only began moving once Barnet helped him, but he should say it awkwardly and concretely rather than narrating his own character arc.

Near the climax, Alve may get one important reflection:

> **Alve:** “Jag trodde också att man kunde fixa allt.”  
> **Alve:** “Det kan man inte.”  
> **Alve:** “Men man kan fixa ganska mycket annat.”

Nova still owns the final realization.

## 5. Act 1 village remains active — locked

The Act 1 village remains a living support network throughout Act 3.

It is not a completed museum/hub that the player has outgrown.

Barnet, Alve and Nova repeatedly travel back across the lake in the restored motorboat to ask the existing residents for help.

The motorboat is therefore an ongoing story/gameplay asset, not a one-scene Act 2 prop.

Resident roles:

- **Henning:** food, event energy, cake, cooking/baking and warm chaos.
- **Linus:** lighting, repairs, construction, troubleshooting and making physical things work.
- **Mira:** furniture, practical items, tools, supplies, sourcing, ordering and logistics.
- **Sol:** music, atmosphere, relationships, emotional reading and small pieces of perspective.

Sol must not become a therapist or explain Nova's story to her.

The fiction remains physical travel by boat even if crossings are mechanically compressed for pacing.

## 6. Macro structure — locked

Act 3 is **one long family-party preparation arc**.

It is explicitly **not four unrelated building projects**.

The four preparation streams all feed the same final family day:

### Henning — food and cake

- ingredients;
- cooking/baking problems;
- cake as a visible payoff.

### Linus — lighting
- practical setup;
- repairing/rigging lights;
- power, fixtures, cables or mounting;
- visible evening transformation.

### Mira — furniture and practical setup
- tables;
- chairs;

- arrangement that feeds Nova's need for perfection.

### Sol — music
- choosing music;
- speakers/playback;
- atmosphere;
- quiet awareness of relational subtext without spoiling the arc.

The tracks may interleave.

Their shared progress fantasy is:

**Make the party better and better until everything feels perfect.**

By the final family day:
- Henning's food/cake is present;
- Linus's lighting works;
- Mira's furniture/setup is in place;
- Sol's music is playing;
- the family day genuinely succeeds.

That success is mandatory.

## 7. Emotional engine — locked

Act 3 intentionally creates tension with the player's learned SysselCraft logic:

**broken things can often be repaired**

therefore a child may believe:

**perhaps a broken family can be repaired too.**

That belief must not be validated.

Series shorthand:
- Act 1: **the world can change**
- Act 2: **the past does not always come back**
- Act 3: **people can choose not to remain together without choosing the child away**

The preferred thematic endpoint is:

**A family can change without ceasing to be a family.**

Do not turn Act 3 into:
- a therapy lesson;
- custody exposition;
- a courtroom story;
- a parent-villain story.

Both parents remain loving parents even though they are no longer a couple.

## 8. Family-day climax — locked

The party/family day must work.

The parents:
- cooperate;
- laugh;
- remember things;
- genuinely have a good time.

The staging should allow Nova and the player to believe, for as long as possible, that her plan might actually work.

The break comes through ordinary logistics, not melodrama.

Preferred locked handoff:

> **Mamman:** “Det här var jättetrevligt.”  
> **Mamman:** “Men nu måste Nova och jag åka hem.”  
> **Mamman:** “Vi ses vid överlämningen nästa vecka.”

No kiss.
No reunion.
No villainous rejection.

Then Nova's key hinge:

> **Nova:** “Men ni hade ju kul.”

This line must land after the event has genuinely succeeded.

## 9. Nova's resolution — locked

Nova leaves the park.

Barnet follows.

Alve initially stays behind so the core resolution belongs to Barnet + Nova.

Nova eventually explains the day she has linked to the separation:
- she had been very difficult/angry;
- she shouted at her father;
- her mother became angry;
- she slammed a door hard enough for a picture to fall;
- later that evening her parents told her that her father would move out.

Core realization:

> **Nova:** “Jag trodde att om det var mitt fel så kunde jag fixa det.”  
> **Nova:** “Jag tror bara att jag önskade att det var mitt fel.”  
> **Nova:** “För då hade jag kunnat göra något åt det.”

Barnet must not answer the emotional question for her.

Nova reaches:

> **Nova:** “Det var deras grej.”  
> **Barnet:** “Ja.”  
> **Nova:** “Inte min.”

Only after Nova reaches that realization does her mother find her.

The mother confirms that Nova did not cause the divorce and could not have prevented it.

Preferred optional final line:

> **Mamman:** “Du behöver aldrig laga oss.”

Nova and her mother hug.

## 10. Visual POV progression — locked

The final family-day sequence uses Nova's subjective visual perspective.

Progression:

**nostalgia -> cold shower -> reality -> new calm**

Before the break:
- warm;
- golden;
- lively;
- parents may appear subtly idealized;
- repairs/setup look almost impossibly perfect.

At the ordinary handoff:
- palette becomes colder/greyer;
- adults look subtly older/tired/more ordinary;
- lighting becomes flatter;
- the same repairs reveal their real handmade imperfections.

Nothing objectively breaks.

After Nova's realization:
- color returns gradually;
- it does **not** return to the earlier idealized gold;
- visible imperfections remain;
- the final world feels safe, real and good rather than magically repaired.

This is a perceptual shift, not a literal environment mutation.
